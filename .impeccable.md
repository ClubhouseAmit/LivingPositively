## Design Context

### Users

People using a mental-health support app in vulnerable or distressed moments.
When they open Metsilon their attention, patience, and emotional bandwidth are
limited — sometimes severely. The job to be done is immediate and concrete:
reach an emergency contact, follow a personal safety plan, or complete one small
reflective act (a gratitude entry, a positive-trait note).

Secondary users are people maintaining wellness habits over time, plus family
and caregiver contexts where trust, privacy, and clear recovery paths matter.

The app ships in Hebrew (RTL), English (LTR), and Arabic (RTL). Hebrew is the
primary locale — RTL is not an afterthought, it is the default reading direction
for most users.

Success means a user can find help, recover from a mistake, understand what the
app is asking, and continue their workflow without confusion, dead ends, or
inaccessible controls.

### Brand Personality

**Calm, supportive, practical.**

Voice: a steady companion, not a clinician and not a cheerleader. Plain language,
short sentences, no jargon, no exclamation marks, no guilt. Never imply the user
has failed at anything.

The interface should feel safe and steady rather than clinical, decorative, or
attention-seeking. It reduces pressure in crisis moments and preserves agency in
reflective flows. Emotional target: **steadied** — the user should feel the app
is holding the structure so they don't have to.

### Aesthetic Direction

Soft, warm, unhurried. The Calm / Headspace family of feeling: generous
whitespace, rounded shapes (20 buttons / 16 cards / 10 inputs), muted pastel
warmth built on the calming violet primary `0xFFA688F8` over the warm off-white
backdrop `0xFFF4F0EB`. Friendly but never childish; nothing saturated, urgent,
or gamified. Rubik throughout, at Medium weight for emphasis rather than Bold.

**Anti-references:**

- Clinical and government health apps — dense forms, sharp corners, saturated
  system blues, bureaucratic tone.
- Text-heavy screens that trap the user before they can act.
- Crisis flows where emergency access is hidden, delayed, or visually secondary.
- Decorative wellness UI that makes standard controls feel unfamiliar.
- Generic polish that improves appearance while leaving recovery, empty states,
  accessibility, or localization gaps unresolved.

**Theme:** Light and dark are both first-class. Dark mode is time-scheduled
(`main.dart` → `usesDarkModeAt`), so a user can cross into it mid-session on any
screen. Every new screen is designed and verified in both themes, and no token
ships without a dark value. The `progressTrack` and `suggestionOutline` gaps
noted in `DESIGN.md` are debt to close, not a precedent to follow.

**Motion:** currently near-absent, and that is a gap rather than a decision.
Motion is welcome where it aids orientation or softens a transition — propose it.
Keep it in character: short, eased, purposeful. Never bouncy, never decorative
for its own sake, and never in the path of an emergency action. Always honor
`MediaQuery.disableAnimations` / reduced-motion.

### Design Principles

1. **Crisis access is never more than one reach away.** Emergency contact access
   stays reachable and visually primary in every app state. No screen, modal, or
   flow may bury it.

2. **One clear next action.** A distressed user gets a single primary action, not
   a wall of choices or a block of text. Never stack more than two CTAs.

3. **Every exit has a way back.** Destructive or dismissive actions get
   confirmation, undo, or a visible recovery path. Error, empty, and loading
   states are designed, not defaulted, and never rely on color alone.

4. **Predictable across every axis.** Behavior holds in Hebrew, Arabic, and
   English; at large text scales; under screen readers; on small screens. Use
   `TextAlign.start/end`, `AlignmentDirectional`, `EdgeInsetsDirectional` — never
   branch manually on text direction. WCAG AA is the floor.

5. **Use the system, don't extend it silently.** Reach for the existing token or
   component before writing a new one. Improvements stay local to existing
   Flutter boundaries unless a human approves architectural change.

### Working Rules

- **Figma vs. tokens: stop and ask.** When a Figma frame specifies a value that
  conflicts with a `DESIGN.md` token, do not silently pick a winner and do not
  invent a new token. Flag the conflict — in the PR description or directly to
  the user — and wait for a decision.
- **Derive spacing, never guess it.** Every gap comes from Figma node
  positions/sizes, measured between container or group nodes (leaf text nodes
  carry auto-height noise). Assert the results with `tester.getRect` in tests.
- **Mirror the design's container tree.** Match the structure, then audit both
  axes. Framework defaults add invisible space — check for it.
- **A user-found defect is a sample, not an incident.** When one is reported,
  sweep the whole category before reporting back.
- **Render before reporting.** Don't claim visual fidelity you haven't looked at.
- **During visual design work, hardcode English strings with `// TODO(l10n)`.**
  Don't run `flutter gen-l10n` mid-pass.
